# What is a PiRogue?

<div style={{textAlign: 'center', margin: '1.5rem 0'}}>
  <img src="/img/logos/pirogue.svg" alt="PiRogue logo" style={{width: '120px', height: '120px'}} />
</div>

The PiRogue is a network router that captures and analyzes network traffic in real time. It started as open hardware built on a Raspberry Pi, and is the foundation of the PiRogue Tool Suite. It now also runs on a regular computer, a virtual machine or a server.

You connect the device you want to examine to the PiRogue, and the PiRogue shows you what that device talks to. It is used for:

* **Network traffic analysis**: deep packet inspection of the traffic to spot suspicious patterns and potential threats, see [Network traffic analysis](/docs/explanation/pirogue/network-traffic-analysis)
* **Mobile device forensics**: the consensual extraction and analysis of the data of a mobile device, such as messages and application data, to reveal signs of compromise, see [Consensual device forensics](/docs/explanation/pirogue/device-forensics)
* **Mobile app analysis**: recording what an app sends, even when the traffic is encrypted, see [Mobile app analysis](/docs/explanation/pirogue/app-analysis)

## In the field and in the lab

The same device serves two very different audiences.

In the field, it is a tool for emergency response. A responder working with people in a repressive environment can isolate a device suspected of active spying or tampering, watch its traffic, and run forensic tools that come pre-installed, without a laboratory.

In the lab, it is an instrument for technical experts, who use it to:

* determine the list of data an app collects
* assess the regulatory compliance of an app
* conduct penetration tests
* analyze the behavior of malware
* produce reproducible analyses and comprehensive reports

## How it fits together

A PiRogue works on its own: its [dashboard](/docs/reference/pirogue/dashboard) shows the results of the analysis without any other tool. It can also send its captures, its experiments and its alerts to [Colander](/docs/explanation/colander/what-is-colander), to turn them into an investigation shared with a team.

How it does so is described in [Architecture](/docs/explanation/pirogue/architecture), and the different ways it can be connected to the devices it analyzes in [Operating modes](/docs/explanation/pirogue/operating-modes).
